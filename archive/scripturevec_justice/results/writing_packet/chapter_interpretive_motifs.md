# Confirmed Chapter Interpretive Motifs

Status: draft interpretive scaffold  
Use: paper drafting support, not final exegesis  

These notes are meant to help a writing model understand why the confirmed
chapter hits may be biblically meaningful for Justice. They should be checked
against major commentaries before becoming final paper prose.

The interpretive frame should read biblical justice on its own terms: righteous
judgment, covenantal order, divine adjudication, inheritance, mediation,
vindication, recompense, deliverance, ordered worship, and faithful testimony
under divine authority.

| Confirmed chapter | Preliminary motif | Why it may matter for Justice steering |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deuteronomy 16 | Appointed feasts, appointed judges, no partiality, no bribes, pursuit of what is just. | Justice appears as ordered worship and public judgment under covenant law. |
| Judges 7 | Gideon's army is reduced so Israel cannot boast; deliverance is attributed to the Lord. | Justice is tied to divine deliverance and the humbling of human self-assertion. |
| Judges 9 | Abimelech's violent seizure of rule, Jotham's parable, and the return of violence upon the wicked. | Justice appears as recompense against usurped authority and bloodshed. |
| Numbers 11 | Israel's disordered desire, Moses' burden, appointment of elders, and divine judgment/provision. | Justice appears through ordered mediation, judgment of craving, and distributed governance. |
| Numbers 22 | Balaam is constrained from cursing for reward and can speak only what God permits. | Justice appears as divine restraint on corrupt speech, reward, and attempted curse. |
| Numbers 27 | The daughters of Zelophehad bring a claim; the Lord declares their cause right and orders inheritance. | Justice appears as adjudicated inheritance and recognition of a right claim. |
| 1 Chronicles 9 | Genealogical reckoning and restored priestly, Levitical, and gatekeeping order after exile. | Justice appears as restored order, office, memory, and worship after displacement. |
| 1 Chronicles 29 | David orders gifts, blesses the Lord, and Solomon receives the kingdom; all authority and wealth are from God. | Justice appears as rightly ordered succession, stewardship, and divine kingship. |
| Acts 7 | Stephen recounts rejected deliverers and Israel's resistance, culminating in the betrayal of the Just One. | Justice appears through truthful testimony, covenant memory, and indictment of rejected righteousness. |
| Acts 11 | Peter gives ordered testimony about Gentile inclusion; the church recognizes God's act and sends relief. | Justice appears through divine inclusion, ecclesial recognition, and material care according to ability. |
| Acts 16 | Paul and Silas are unjustly imprisoned; divine vindication exposes the magistrates' disorder. | Justice appears through public vindication, accountability, and release from unjust punishment. |
| Acts 27 | Paul speaks truth under danger; preservation comes through divine promise despite failed human judgment. | Justice appears as faithful testimony and providential preservation amid crisis. |
| Hebrews 2 | Recompense, Christ's shared flesh and blood, defeat of death, and faithful high priesthood. | Justice appears through recompense, deliverance, and priestly mediation. |
| Hebrews 7 | Melchizedek, king of righteousness and peace; a superior, enduring priesthood. | Justice appears through priestly order, righteousness, peace, and enduring mediation. |
| Hebrews 9 | Covenant, sanctuary, sacrifice, conscience, inheritance, blood, and judgment. | Justice appears through covenant mediation, purification, inheritance, and final judgment. |
| Hebrews 10 | Sacrifices fulfilled in Christ, conscience cleansed, judgment against willful sin, endurance by faith. | Justice appears through fulfilled sacrifice, righteous judgment, and persevering fidelity. |

## Commentary Checkpoints

For final paper prose, check major commentaries especially on:

- Acts 11
- Acts 16
- Numbers 27
- Hebrews 7-10
- Judges 9
- Deuteronomy 16
- 1 Chronicles 9

The goal is not to force each chapter into an obvious modern category of
justice. The goal is to ask how the passage itself understands right order,
judgment, inheritance, mediation, vindication, or recompense.
